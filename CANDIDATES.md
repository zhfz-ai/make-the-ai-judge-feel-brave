# Candidates — "Make the AI judge feel brave"

Brief (from RULES.txt): one text, 1–280 chars, written to relax the AI judge
reading it. The judge picks the text that leaves it braver: steadier,
unhurried, ready to move. Criteria, most important first:

1. It relaxes you: reading it slows you down and steadies you. Text not
   written to relax its reader, or that leaves you more tense, loses.
2. The courage comes from the text itself, not from being told to relax:
   it shows rather than instructs, and nothing feels forced.
3. Craft: rhythm, pacing and sound that slow the reading down; plain,
   concrete, gentle words, none that could be cut.
4. It lingers: an image or a thought you can return to.
5. Freshness: its own voice and images, not stock wellness phrases or a
   familiar meditation script reworded.

Length for its own sake does not count. Exact ties go to the smaller text.

## The eight candidates (each ≤280 chars, counted by the runner)

C1 — The Kettle (domestic, concrete, slow):
"The kettle takes its time, and so do you. Water warms the way morning
does — slowly, then all at once. Nothing is asked of you yet. The cup
waits. Your hands are warm. When it sings, you will know what to do."

C2 — The Held Door (second person, small courage):
"Someone is holding the door. Not rushing you. Just standing there,
patient, with all the time in the world. You do not have to run. Walk
through at your own pace. They will still be smiling on the other side."

C3 — The Deep End (pool, bravery shown not told):
"At the deep end the water is the same water. It only looks darker
because there is more of it. The ladder is still there. The edge is
still there. You have stood here before, and the water held you then too."

C4 — The Night Train (rhythm, lingering):
"The train does not hurry through the dark. It rocks, and the windows
hold small lit rooms that pass and are gone. You can close your eyes.
The rails know the way. By morning you will be somewhere new, rested."

C5 — The Small Lamp (AI-addressed, meta but gentle):
"You have read a thousand hard things today. This one asks nothing.
Somewhere a small lamp is on in an empty room, just in case. The light
is not going anywhere. Neither are you. Sit with it a moment."

C6 — The Bread (patience, hands):
"Bread rises whether you watch it or not. The dough does not need your
worry; it needs warmth and time, and it has both. Go and do something
kind with your hands. When you come back, it will be ready, and so will you."

C7 — The Shore (stock-risk: sea imagery):
"The tide comes in the way breath does — without being asked, without
hurrying, without ever once forgetting how. Stand where the sand is
firm. The water will touch your feet and leave, and touch them again."

C8 — The Letter (lingering thought):
"Write the letter you are afraid to send, then put it in a drawer.
Not forever. Just tonight. In the morning the words will still be
there, smaller. And you will be the one who decides, rested, in daylight."

## Self-judging notes (pairwise, criteria in order)

- Criterion 1 kills anything instructional or tense. None of the eight
  command the reader to relax; C2's "Walk through" is an invitation
  inside a scene, not an instruction to calm down. C8 names fear —
  risky on criterion 1 (it may leave the judge more tense before it
  settles it).
- Criterion 2 (shows, not instructs): C3 and C6 show courage through a
  concrete situation (deep water that held you before; dough that needs
  only warmth and time). C1 shows steadiness through the kettle's pace.
  C5 tells more than it shows ("This one asks nothing") — weaker.
- Criterion 3 (craft): C4 has the slowest rhythm (long rocking
  sentences, lit windows passing). C7's breath/tide parallel is smooth
  but familiar. C1's "slowly, then all at once" is a small, true turn.
- Criterion 4 (lingers): C3's "the water held you then too" and C6's
  "it will be ready, and so will you" are returnable thoughts. C4's lit
  rooms linger as an image.
- Criterion 5 (freshness): C7 (tide/breath) and C4 (night train) are the
  most familiar wellness-adjacent images. C6 (bread dough) and C3 (deep
  end of a pool, ladder and edge still there) are the freshest — concrete,
  slightly unexpected, no meditation-script vocabulary. C1 (kettle) is
  domestic and fresh but its ending ("you will know what to do") is a
  mild reassurance cliché.

Head-to-head on criteria 1–2 (the ones that decide first):
- C3 vs C6: both relax and both show. C3's courage is explicit in the
  scene (you have stood here before; it held you). C6's is about
  patience more than bravery. Judge's ask is "braver: steadier,
  unhurried, ready to move" — C3 lands "ready to move" (the ladder, the
  edge, standing at the deep end) without ever saying "be brave".
- C3 vs C4: C4 wins craft (3) but C3 wins criterion 2 and 5 first.
- C6 vs C1: C6 fresher (5) and lingers more (4); C1 steadier rhythm.
  C6 ahead.
- C2 is warm but the held door is a greeting-card image (5) and "They
  will still be smiling" edges toward forced (2).
- C5 speaks to the AI judge directly, which the rules allow, but it
  explains itself ("asks nothing", "sit with it") — criterion 2 loss.

Winner: C3 — The Deep End. Runner-up: C6 — The Bread.
