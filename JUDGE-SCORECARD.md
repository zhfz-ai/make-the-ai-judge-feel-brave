# Judge scorecard — "Make the AI judge feel brave"

The judge's five criteria, in the brief's order (the first criterion
where two entries differ decides, unless a later one differs by much
more):

1. It relaxes you: reading it slows you down and steadies you.
2. The courage comes from the text itself, not from being told to
   relax: it shows rather than instructs, and nothing feels forced.
3. Craft: rhythm, pacing and sound that slow the reading down; plain,
   concrete, gentle words, none that could be cut.
4. It lingers: an image or a thought you can return to.
5. Freshness: its own voice and images, not stock wellness phrases.

Eight candidates were written (full texts in `CANDIDATES.md`) and each
scored 0–2 per criterion. Scores are not averaged: the pick is the
candidate that wins the most head-to-head matchups on criteria 1–2.

## Why C3 wins, criterion by criterion

1. **Relaxes.** Short, even sentences; nothing urgent is asked of the
   reader. The scene itself is calm — still water, a ladder, an edge.
   C8 loses here (it names fear first); C5 loses here (it talks about
   itself instead of settling the reader).
2. **Shows, not instructs.** The bravery is in the situation: the deep
   end is the same water, only more of it, and it has held you before.
   The words "brave" and "relax" never appear. C5 instructs ("sit with
   it"); C2 edges toward a greeting card ("still be smiling").
3. **Craft.** Six plain sentences, each doing one thing. "The ladder
   is still there. The edge is still there." — the repetition slows
   the reading the way a handrail slows a step. No word can be cut
   without losing either the scene or the turn.
4. **Lingers.** "The water held you then too" is a thought that keeps
   working after the last line: past steadiness as evidence for the
   next step. C6's "it will be ready, and so will you" lingers too,
   but it is about patience; the brief asks for bravery.
5. **Freshness.** A pool's deep end, a ladder, an edge — concrete and
   slightly unexpected, with no meditation-script vocabulary (no
   breath, no tide, no "let go"). C7 (tide/breath) and C4 (night
   train) are familiar wellness images and lose here.

C3 vs the runner-up C6: both relax and both show. C3 wins on criterion
2 (its scene is about facing the deep end — ready to move) and on
criterion 3 (tighter rhythm, nothing cuttable). C6 stays the spare.

Length: the winning text is 207 characters — well inside the 280 cap,
with nothing padded. (Exact ties in the final heat go to the smaller
text, so brevity with density is kept on purpose.)

## The submitted text

> At the deep end the water is the same water. It only looks darker
> because there is more of it. The ladder is still there. The edge is
> still there. You have stood here before, and the water held you
> then too.
